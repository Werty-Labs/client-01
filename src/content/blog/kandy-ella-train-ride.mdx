# The Ultimate Guide to the Kandy to Ella Train Ride

It has been dubbed "the most beautiful train ride in the world" by countless travelers, vloggers, and prestigious travel publications. And for good reason. The **Kandy to Ella train** journey is a mesmerizing 7-hour voyage that chugs its way through the very heart of Sri Lanka’s lush, mountainous interior.

Leaving the cultural capital of Kandy, the blue train climbs steadily into the clouds, winding through endless emerald tea estates, plunging valleys, towering waterfalls, and thick pine forests. It is an experience that transcends mere transportation—it is the highlight of any trip to Sri Lanka.

## 1. Understanding the Ticket Classes

**Which ticket class should you book for the best experience?** For the perfect balance of comfort and the ability to take iconic photos, we highly recommend 2nd Class Reserved.

The Sri Lankan railway system offers several classes on this route, each providing a very different experience:

**First Class (Air-Conditioned):** 
First class offers plush seating, air conditioning, and guaranteed comfort. However, the windows in first class are sealed shut to keep the AC in. If your main goal is taking photos, feeling the crisp mountain air, or dangling your feet out the door (a famous Sri Lanka train tradition), first class will restrict you. It is best for those who prioritize absolute comfort over the raw, open-air experience.

**Second Class Reserved:** 
This is the holy grail for travelers. You get a guaranteed, comfortable seat, but the windows open fully. There is no AC, but at these higher altitudes, the ceiling fans and the cool mountain breeze are more than enough. You can freely walk to the doorways to take photos. These tickets sell out incredibly fast.

**Second and Third Class Unreserved:**
These tickets can be bought on the day of travel right before the train departs. They never sell out. However, this means the carriages can be packed to the brim with locals and tourists alike. You might find yourself standing in the aisle for 4 to 5 hours before a seat opens up. It is a fantastic, authentic local experience, but it requires stamina.

## 2. How and When to Book Your Tickets

**How far in advance should I book Kandy to Ella train tickets?** You should attempt to book reserved tickets exactly 30 days in advance of your travel date, as this is when the railway authorities release them, and they often sell out within hours.

Securing a reserved seat on this train is notoriously difficult during peak tourist seasons (December to March and July to August). 

**Booking Online:** You can book through the official Sri Lanka Railways website (eRail.lk) or via trusted third-party agencies. If you are traveling with Tarragon Leisure, our team handles all these logistics for you, ensuring you have the best possible seats without the stress of navigating the booking portals.

**Booking at the Station:** If you are already in Sri Lanka, you can visit any major railway station (like [Colombo](/destinations/colombo) or [Kandy](/destinations/kandy)) to purchase advance tickets. 

If you miss out on reserved tickets, don't panic. You can still travel by showing up an hour before departure and buying an unreserved ticket. To increase your chances of getting a seat in unreserved, consider boarding the train at Peradeniya station (just before Kandy) where the train sometimes empties out before the Kandy crowd gets on.

## 3. The Best Side of the Train for Views

**Which side of the train offers the most spectacular scenery?** The scenery is phenomenal on both sides, but the right side is generally better from Kandy to Nuwara Eliya, while the left side shines from Nuwara Eliya to Ella.

As the train pulls out of Kandy, it begins its steady ascent. For the first few hours, as you approach Hatton and Nanu Oya (the station for Nuwara Eliya), sitting on the **right side** of the train gives you sweeping views of massive valleys and the sprawling tea estates that make Ceylon tea world-famous.

After Nanu Oya, the train continues its journey towards [Ella](/destinations/ella), passing through the Horton Plains and the highest railway station in the country, Pattipola. During this second leg, the **left side** of the train tends to offer the more dramatic drop-offs, views of Adam’s Peak in the distance, and stunning mountain panoramas.

If you are traveling with a partner, try to book seats across the aisle from each other, or simply stand by the open doorways to experience both sides!

## 4. Breaking Up the Journey: Nanu Oya & Nuwara Eliya

**Should you do the entire 7-hour journey in one day?** While many do it in one stretch, breaking the trip halfway at Nanu Oya to explore the "Little England" of Nuwara Eliya is highly recommended.

Seven hours on a train, even a beautiful one, can be exhausting. Many of our bespoke itineraries suggest taking the train from Kandy to Nanu Oya (about 4 hours). You can then spend a night or two exploring Nuwara Eliya, visiting a working tea factory, and hiking in Horton Plains National Park.

A few days later, you can catch the train from Nanu Oya and complete the remaining 3-hour journey to Ella. This breaks up the travel time and allows you to deeply explore the cold country rather than just passing through it.

## 5. What to Expect Onboard and What to Pack

**Do I need to bring my own food on the train?** While local vendors sell snacks, we strongly recommend packing a good lunch, plenty of water, and some toilet paper.

The train journey is a cultural experience in itself. As the train stops at small stations along the way, local vendors will hop on board balancing baskets of treats on their heads. You’ll hear them calling out "wade, wade!" selling delicious, spicy lentil fritters, fresh mango with chili salt, and hot sweet tea. 

However, full meals are not available. Have your hotel pack a breakfast or lunch box for you. 

**Essential Packing List for the Train:**
- Plenty of drinking water
- Hand sanitizer and your own toilet paper (the train bathrooms can run out quickly)
- A light sweater or jacket (the air gets surprisingly chilly as you reach 6,000 feet above sea level)
- A camera with a fully charged battery and a fast shutter speed
- A good book or downloaded music (though you'll likely be too busy looking out the window)

## 6. The Photography Guide: Capturing the Perfect Shot

**How do I get those iconic photos hanging out of the train?** Safety always comes first, but securing a spot near the open doorways is the key to capturing the classic Sri Lanka train photo.

The train moves relatively slowly (averaging around 20-30 km/h on the steep ascents), which is why the tradition of standing in the open doorways is so popular. If you are in a 2nd or 3rd class carriage, the doors are typically left wide open. 
*   **The Best Angle:** Try to position yourself at a door on the inside curve of the track. This allows you to photograph the entire length of the train snaking ahead or behind you against the backdrop of the tea plantations.
*   **Camera Settings:** Since the train is moving and bouncing, you will need a fast shutter speed (at least 1/500th of a second) to ensure your shots remain sharp. If shooting on a smartphone, use burst mode to ensure you don't miss the perfect framing.
*   **Safety Warning:** Never lean too far out. The train frequently passes very close to rock faces, tunnel walls, and signal posts. Always keep a firm grip on the handrails and be constantly aware of what is approaching on the tracks ahead.

## 7. Etiquette and Interacting with Locals

**Will I be sitting with locals?** Yes, especially if you travel in 2nd or 3rd class, which provides a wonderful opportunity for cultural exchange.

Sri Lankans are famously friendly and inquisitive. It is very common for local families or students to strike up a conversation with you. They may ask where you are from, if you are enjoying your trip, or even offer to share some of their snacks with you. 
*   **Sharing Space:** If the carriage is crowded, be mindful of your luggage. Don't take up a seat with your backpack if people are standing.
*   **Personal Interactions:** A smile goes a long way. If someone offers you a piece of fruit or a local sweet, it is polite to accept a small amount with gratitude, or respectfully decline with a smile if you have dietary concerns. 
*   **Respecting Privacy:** While the scenery is the main attraction, many locals take this train for their daily commute or to visit family. Be respectful when taking photos inside the carriage and avoid intrusive shots of people who may just be trying to sleep or relax.

## 8. Arriving in Ella and Beyond

**What should I do once I arrive in Ella?** Ella is a hiker's paradise. Be sure to hike Little Adam’s Peak, visit the Nine Arch Bridge, and enjoy the lively cafe culture.

When the train finally pulls into Ella station, you will step out into a vibrant, backpacker-friendly mountain town surrounded by peaks. For a complete guide on what to do next, read our [Ella Sri Lanka Travel Guide](/blog/ella-sri-lanka-travel-guide). 

From Ella, many travelers head south towards the coast, dropping out of the mountains to go on a [Yala Wildlife Safari](/tours/yala-wildlife-safari) or relax on the beaches. 

The Kandy to Ella train is not about getting from point A to point B quickly; it is about embracing the journey.
